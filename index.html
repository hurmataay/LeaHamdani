Four briefs, four audiences

Each project started with a client who had one specific problem: nobody knew them, nobody trusted them, or nobody had time to explain their own system. The strategy changed each time; the questions underneath didn't.

01

Turning a $5 gift into something a college student would actually give

Marketing Strategist · charity: water · Global Career Accelerator
Audience segmentationOrganic socialPaid + retargeting

The problem: college students already care about causes like clean water, but a donation still reads as an adult commitment they can't afford to make.

The approach: reframe monthly giving as something closer to a dorm tradition than a financial decision. Lea built the organic content pillars, the interest based paid social concept and copy, and a retargeting layer aimed specifically at people who'd clicked through and stalled before donating.

Paid social creative, aimed at warm, interest based traffic
ResultTop evaluation (A grade) from Global Career Accelerator instructors on the finished campaign.
02

Building a pain management practice a following, starting from zero

Social Media Marketing Manager, Freelance · The Small Marketing Agency
Content strategyOrganic, no ad spend

The problem: a healthcare client needed a real following with a low starting follower count and no budget for ads, which meant every post had to earn its own reach.

The approach: Lea built a four pillar system, informational, testimonials, brand awareness, and creative or viral formats, where each pillar answered one specific question a prospective patient would be silently asking. She mapped it into a sample three week rotation so the client could see the system working before committing further.

ResultA structured, repeatable content plan and posting cadence the client can run without paid support, built from a content audit across social, email, and web.

03

Teaching 66,000 employees to use a system that had never had a tutorial

Adoption Content Creator Intern · Centene
ScriptwritingVideo productionInternal comms

The problem: Centene's internal Jobs Hub platform had no onboarding material, and support teams were fielding the same questions on repeat.

The approach: as the first hire into a newly created role, Lea analyzed recurring support tickets to find where employees actually got stuck, then scoped, scripted, and revised a full tutorial video from a rough draft to a polished, distributed script. She learned Camtasia specifically for the project, recorded and edited the video herself, and documented the entire workflow as a playbook so the process could be repeated for future tutorials.

"Create an instructional YouTube tutorial video for Centene's internal Jobs Hub platform to guide employees on effectively using the platform for job searching and applications... By documenting the workflow, rationale, and lessons learned, the organization ensures continuous improvement in future projects." — from the project playbook, written by Lea Hamdani, August 2023
ResultPresented directly to Centene's Head of HR and adopted company wide, reducing how often employees needed to go through support for basic platform questions.
04

Finding out a kindness program's real problem was that nobody had heard of it

Account Head · Pats for Patriots, Amplify Agency (GMU Student PR Agency)
Primary researchCampaign strategyBroadcast writing

The problem: a campus kindness recognition program had a strong mission but almost no nominations, run mostly by volunteer faculty with limited time for promotion.

The approach: Lea led a 17 question primary research survey across a seven person student agency, which surfaced the real issue: 77% of respondents had never heard of the program, and for many, the survey itself was their first exposure to it. She used that finding to anchor a campaign around QR codes in high traffic campus spots, a student ambassador program, and a radio PSA she wrote and directed for the campus station, WGMU.

One of the campaign posters
ResultA campaign strategy targeting a 50% increase in program visibility and a 40% increase in nominations by the end of the semester, built entirely on what the research actually showed.

The QR code removed the biggest friction point the survey turned up: nobody wants to hunt for a nomination form. This let students submit one in the time it took to walk past a poster.
